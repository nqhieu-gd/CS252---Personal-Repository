- Question: The translation is taking a bit too much time, more than that of a google translate, I think it's not the connection issues
=> You spotted a real architectural bottleneck. First, because `py-googletrans` is a Python library rather than a browser SDK, every request travels Browser -> FastAPI -> `httpx` -> Google Translate instead of talking directly over Google's pre-warmed browser session. Second — and the biggest cause — our `GoogleTrans4Adapter` previously used `async with Translator(...)` on every single call, which created and closed a brand-new `httpx.AsyncClient` every time and paid a fresh DNS + TCP + TLS + HTTP/2 handshake (~2,400–2,680 ms per uncached call in live benchmarks, compared to ~475–765 ms on a warm persistent client). We just fixed this in `backend/app/services/adapter.py` by pooling persistent HTTP/2 `Translator` clients per endpoint preset (and raising bulk concurrency from 3 to 6 so multi-field Destination Guides translate in a single parallel wave), cutting uncached translation latency by ~3x while cached repeats still take <1 ms.
- Question: The translating function only works well for english, almost any other languages either give false meaning or translate from english meaning (i.e. "watashi no namae wa nani desu ka?" means "Tên tôi là gì?" but is translated to "Tên tôi có phải là người Nhật không?")
=> Non-English languages written in their native scripts actually translate accurately (verified live: Japanese `私の名前は何ですか？` -> `"tên tôi là gì?"`, French `Où est la gare la plus proche ?` -> `"Ga xe lửa gần nhất ở đâu?"`, Korean `이 요리에 땅콩이 들어 있나요?` -> `"Có đậu phộng trong món ăn này không?"`, Russian `Где находится ближайшая аптека?` -> `"Hiệu thuốc gần nhất ở đâu?"`). The distortion happens specifically when non-Latin or tonal languages are typed in plain ASCII/Latin phonetic letters (Romaji, un-toned Pinyin, unaccented Vietnamese) without an Input Method Editor (IME). Inspecting Google's raw `extra_data["possible-mistakes"]` payload for `"watashi no namae wa nani desu ka?"` reveals what Google did under the hood: `['', '私 の 名前 和 名に です か？', [6], None, None, True]`. Because spaced Romaji is full of homophones, Google's statistical Romaji-to-Kanji converter misread the topic particle `"wa"` (`は`) as the Kanji `和` (*Wa* = "Japanese / Japanese-style") and `"nani"` (`何`) as `名に` ("for a name"), turning the sentence into `私の名前和名にですか？` -> English pivot `"Is my name Japanese?"` -> Vietnamese `"Tên tôi có phải là người Nhật không?"`. Typing native Japanese Kana/Kanji (`私の名前は何ですか？` or `わたしのなまえはなんですか？`) bypasses that Romaji guesser completely, and Google's converted script is surfaced in the "Did you mean" banner whenever phonetic conversion occurs so you can see and fix misconverted Kanji immediately.
